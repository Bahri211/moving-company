What shapes the price of an Arizona move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on an Arizona move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Arizona lanes run from about 300 miles to Nevada out to roughly 2,160 miles to Florida. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Packing everything yourself is the cheapest option and full packing is the most expensive; most customers land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Moving from Arizona: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes an Arizona move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-10 carries the bulk of Arizona freight east to El Paso and west to Los Angeles; I-17 climbs to Flagstaff and meets I-40 for cross-country runs. The Valley's newer master-planned suburbs are easy truck access, but the older Phoenix neighborhoods — Encanto, Willo, Coronado — have narrow streets and mature trees that force a shuttle. Flagstaff and the Rim country sit above 6,000 feet, and winter deliveries there can require chains on I-17. Main corridors: I-10, I-17, I-40, I-8, US-60.

STEP 02

Timing your move

Summer in the Valley is the constraint that defines Arizona moving. From late May through September, loading happens at dawn; crews stop by early afternoon, and we never leave electronics, candles, vinyl records, or pressurized items in a trailer parked in the sun. October through April is close to ideal moving weather and, conveniently, is also when snowbird traffic pushes demand up — book early for January and March.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Maricopa County has been adding population faster than almost any other county in the US, and the largest single inbound stream is still Southern California — households trading coastal housing costs for Chandler, Gilbert, and Surprise. Outbound Arizona moves have grown too, mostly to Texas and Idaho, driven by the same cost math one step further. Tucson runs its own pattern, with a strong retiree inflow from the upper Midwest each fall.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Arizona are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-18020728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to Arizona's state motor carrier authority, which licenses intrastate household-goods movers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

What catches people out in Arizona.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Arizona move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Heat-sensitive inventory

Trailer interiors in Phoenix exceed 150°F in July. Candles, electronics, medications, artwork, musical instruments, and anything with adhesive or vinyl should be flagged so we move them in a climate-controlled compartment or advise you to carry them.

02

55-plus community rules

Sun City, Sun City West, Sun Lakes, and most Green Valley communities restrict move hours, ban weekend truck traffic, and require gate registration days ahead. We file that paperwork, but it needs lead time.

03

Snowbird two-address moves

A large share of Arizona moves are seasonal, with part of the household going into storage and part going north. We can split an inventory across a delivery and a storage-in-transit lot on the same contract.
