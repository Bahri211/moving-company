Pricing

How a Virginia move gets priced.

There is no price table on this page on purpose. A figure quoted before anyone has seen your household is a guess, and guesses are what become revised invoices at the far end. What follows is what genuinely shifts the cost of a Virginia move — each line itemized in writing before you sign, and binding once you do.

01Distance and lane
Your route sets the floor. Our Virginia lanes run from about 290 miles to North Carolina out to roughly 2,670 miles to California. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.
02Access at both ends
Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.
03How much packing you want
Full packing is what most long-distance customers choose, and it is the single biggest factor in whether a load arrives intact — a professionally packed box is built for a 2,000-mile ride, not a car trip. Most land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.
04Storage between dates
Closing dates and lease ends seldom meet neatly. When there is a gap, your inventory sits in storage-in-transit until the new place is ready — priced in the original quote, not billed as a running surprise. Anything wooden, leather, or electronic belongs in a climate-controlled unit.
05Valuation and protection
The basic released-value coverage the federal rules require comes with every interstate shipment and costs nothing. Above it sits Full Value Protection, which pays to repair, replace, or settle in cash at today's market value — a line worth adding when the inventory is valuable.
Local notes

Moving from Virginia: what's actually different.

The practical detail changes by state — where the trucks can go, when the weather cooperates, who licenses whom. Here is how that plays out in Virginia.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-95 north–south is heavily congested through Northern Virginia, and I-64, I-81, and I-66 handle the rest. Arlington and Alexandria high-rises require certificates of insurance, reserved freight elevators, and often weekday-only moves; some Old Town Alexandria streets are too narrow for a trailer. Hampton Roads adds tunnel constraints — the Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel and Monitor-Merrimac restrict certain vehicles and back up badly. Main corridors: I-95, I-64, I-81, I-66, I-495.

STEP 02

Timing your move

Peak is May through August, intensified by military PCS season and the federal fiscal calendar. Late August adds university turnover in Charlottesville and Blacksburg. Hurricane season affects Hampton Roads from June through November, with tidal flooding a recurring issue in Norfolk independent of major storms. Fall is the best statewide window; winter is quiet, with ice storms the main risk on I-81.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Virginia runs close to balance with a slight outflow. Northern Virginia gains federal and tech relocations while losing households to Maryland, Pennsylvania, and the Carolinas on cost. Hampton Roads volume is dominated by military rotation, which is largely cyclical rather than net. Richmond and Charlottesville have grown steadily. Southwest Virginia and the coalfields continue to lose population.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Virginia are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles Motor Carrier Services — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

Good to know

The Virginia details that cost people money.

These three catch people out in Virginia often enough that we look for all of them at survey — which is how they end up in the quote instead of on the final invoice.

01

Military PCS documentation

Hampton Roads and Northern Virginia both generate heavy PCS volume. Those moves need certified weight tickets, fixed delivery dates, and documentation matched to reimbursement requirements, all of which we provide.

02

Tunnel restrictions in Hampton Roads

The Hampton Roads Bridge-Tunnel and Monitor-Merrimac restrict certain vehicles and cargo and are frequent congestion points. Routing around them changes timing more than distance suggests.

03

Norfolk tidal flooding

Parts of Norfolk and Virginia Beach flood on high tides without any storm. We check tide tables when scheduling those moves rather than treating flooding as a storm-only risk.
