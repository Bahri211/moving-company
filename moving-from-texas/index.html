What shapes the price of a Texas move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Texas move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Texas lanes run from about 720 miles to Tennessee out to roughly 1,450 miles to California. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Packing everything yourself is the cheapest option and full packing is the most expensive; most customers land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Moving from Texas: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Texas move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-35, I-45, I-10, and I-20 form the state's skeleton, and Texas distances are genuinely large — Dallas to El Paso is over 600 miles without leaving the state. Suburban access in Frisco, Katy, Plano, and The Woodlands is straightforward, though most master-planned communities require gate registration and a certificate of insurance. Downtown Austin, Houston, and Dallas high-rises need reserved freight elevators and dock time slots. Main corridors: I-35, I-45, I-10, I-20, I-30.

STEP 02

Timing your move

Peak is May through August, which is also when Texas heat is most punishing — crews start at dawn and heat-sensitive items get flagged. Gulf Coast moves face hurricane season from June through November, and we monitor named storms against Houston and Corpus Christi delivery windows. Winter is mild and quiet, and the easiest time of year to get the date you want, with the caveat that rare hard freezes can shut down highways for a day.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Texas leads the country in net inbound migration, and the largest single source is California, followed by Florida, Illinois, New York, and Louisiana. Austin, Dallas–Fort Worth, and Houston absorb the bulk of it, with San Antonio and the I-35 corridor between it and Austin growing fast. Outbound Texas volume has grown as well, going to Oklahoma, Tennessee, Colorado, and Florida, often on cost and climate.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Texas are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-18020728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles Motor Carrier Division, which licenses intrastate household-goods movers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

What catches people out in Texas.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Texas move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Summer heat handling

Trailer interiors across Texas exceed 140°F in July. Electronics, candles, artwork, vinyl, and medications should be flagged at survey so they travel climate-controlled or with you.

02

Master-planned community rules

Most of the DFW, Houston, and Austin suburbs are governed by HOAs requiring gate registration and a certificate of insurance. Approvals take a few days and are the most common cause of avoidable delay on a Texas move.

03

Getting to the state line

Texas is large enough that the run from your door to the state line is a real leg of the job. An El Paso pickup heading east covers roughly 550 miles before it even leaves Texas, so we schedule origin-side transit as part of the plan rather than an afterthought.
