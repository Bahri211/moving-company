What shapes the price of a Nebraska move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Nebraska move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Nebraska lanes run from about 410 miles to Missouri out to roughly 1,540 miles to California. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Packing everything yourself is the cheapest option and full packing is the most expensive; most customers land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Moving from Nebraska: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Nebraska move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-80 spans the state and makes east–west routing simple; north–south movement relies on US highways and takes longer than the map suggests. Omaha and Lincoln suburbs have easy truck access. Rural moves involve long gravel drives and county roads that soften in spring. Western Nebraska distances are substantial — Scottsbluff is over 400 miles from Omaha — and that origin leg is a real cost factor. Main corridors: I-80, I-680, US-81, US-83.

STEP 02

Timing your move

Peak is May through August, driven partly by military PCS season and partly by the school calendar and the University of Nebraska turnover in Lincoln. Winter is quiet but Nebraska gets genuine ground blizzards on I-80 that close the interstate for hours at a time. Spring brings severe weather and soft rural roads. September and October are the state's best moving window.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Nebraska runs a modest net outflow, mainly younger residents leaving for Denver, the Twin Cities, Dallas, and Phoenix. Omaha itself has grown steadily and pulls households in from rural Nebraska and western Iowa. The Sandhills and western counties continue to lose population. Offutt AFB generates predictable PCS volume that spikes from May through July.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Nebraska are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Nebraska Public Service Commission, which regulates intrastate household-goods carriers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

What catches people out in Nebraska.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Nebraska move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

I-80 consolidation advantage

Because so much cross-country freight runs I-80 through Nebraska, we can usually place a shipment on a passing consolidated load quickly, which keeps shared-load pricing competitive relative to distance.

02

Offutt AFB PCS moves

Military relocations cluster May through July with fixed report dates. We run those dedicated and provide the weight tickets and documentation reimbursement requires.

03

Ground blizzard closures

Nebraska's characteristic winter hazard is blowing snow closing I-80, sometimes with clear skies overhead. It is usually a matter of hours, but it is why winter windows include a contingency day.
