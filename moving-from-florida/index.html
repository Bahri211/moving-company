Pricing

What shapes the price of a Florida move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Florida move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01Distance and lane
Your route sets the floor. Our Florida lanes run from about 470 miles to Georgia out to roughly 2,570 miles to California. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.
02Access at both ends
Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.
03How much packing you want
Full packing is what most long-distance customers choose, and it is the single biggest factor in whether a load arrives intact — a professionally packed box is built for a 2,000-mile ride, not a car trip. Most land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.
04Storage between dates
Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.
05Valuation and protection
Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.
Local notes

Moving from Florida: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Florida move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-95 on the east coast, I-75 on the west, and I-4 across the middle carry nearly all Florida moving traffic, and I-4 through Orlando is among the most congested corridors in the Southeast. The defining access issue is vertical: condo towers from Miami Beach to Naples require a certificate of insurance, a reserved freight elevator, and often a move window restricted to weekday mornings. Gated and 55-plus communities add registration requirements on top of that. Main corridors: I-95, I-75, I-4, I-10, Florida's Turnpike.

STEP 02

Timing your move

The moving calendar here is inverted relative to most of the country. Winter and early spring are peak season as snowbirds and retirees arrive; June through November is hurricane season, when we build flexibility into coastal delivery windows and will not run a loaded trailer into a landfall forecast. Late spring — April and May — is often the best combination of weather and availability in Florida.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Florida's inbound flow is enormous and comes primarily from New York, New Jersey, Illinois, Pennsylvania, and Ohio, concentrated in Tampa Bay, Southwest Florida, and Central Florida. What gets less attention is outbound volume: rising insurance premiums, HOA assessments, and coastal housing costs have pushed a steady stream of Florida households toward Texas, Tennessee, the Carolinas, and Georgia. The Villages and Southwest Florida remain overwhelmingly net inbound; South Florida is closer to balanced.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Florida are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS), which registers intrastate household-goods movers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

Good to know

What catches people out in Florida.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Florida move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Condo and HOA approval

Most Florida towers require an approved COI, a reserved elevator, and sometimes an association-approved move date. Approval can take a week or more. This is the single most common source of Florida move delays.

02

Hurricane-season contingencies

From June through November we track named storms against your route and dates. We would rather hold a load 48 hours than deliver into an evacuation zone, and your contract accounts for that.

03

Humidity and long-term storage

Standard warehouse storage in Florida is hard on leather, wood, and upholstery. If your goods will be stored more than a few weeks, climate control is worth asking for.
