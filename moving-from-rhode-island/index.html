Pricing

What shapes the price of a Rhode Island move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Rhode Island move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Rhode Island lanes run from about 50 miles to Massachusetts out to roughly 1,780 miles to Texas. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Full packing is what most long-distance customers choose, and it is the single biggest factor in whether a load arrives intact — a professionally packed box is built for a 2,000-mile ride, not a car trip. Most land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Local notes

Moving from Rhode Island: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Rhode Island move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-95 runs the length of the state and I-195 connects to southeastern Massachusetts. The difficulty is entirely local: Providence's East Side and Federal Hill have narrow streets with parking on both sides, triple-deckers with tight turning stairs, and few driveways. Newport's colonial district has streets too narrow for a full-size trailer, and summer tourist traffic makes daytime loading there impractical. Main corridors: I-95, I-195, I-295, RI-4.

STEP 02

Timing your move

Peak is May through August, and the last week of August is difficult statewide because of Brown, RISD, and URI turnover. Newport should be avoided in July and August if you have any choice — tourist congestion effectively closes the historic district to trucks during the day. September and October are ideal. Winter is quiet, with nor'easters and coastal flooding the main risks.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Rhode Island has a modest net outflow, concentrated in retirees heading to Florida and the Carolinas and younger residents leaving for Boston. It gains households from Massachusetts, particularly around the Attleboro and Seekonk line where Rhode Island housing costs less. Newport runs a distinct seasonal and second-home market, and the coastal south county towns have gained remote workers.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Rhode Island are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Rhode Island Division of Public Utilities and Carriers, which licenses intrastate movers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

Good to know

What catches people out in Rhode Island.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Rhode Island move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Triple-decker staircases

Providence and Pawtucket triple-deckers have narrow turning stairs that will not clear a large sectional or box spring. We plan disassembly or a hoist at survey rather than improvising on the day.

02

Newport summer access

The colonial street grid combined with July and August tourist traffic makes daytime truck staging in Newport genuinely impractical. Early morning or off-season is the workable option.

03

University turnover week

Brown, RISD, Providence College, and URI all turn over within days of each other in late August. Capacity across the state disappears that week.
