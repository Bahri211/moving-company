Long-Distance Movers in Colorado

Colorado moves are shaped by altitude and by the I-25 corridor, where nearly 85 percent of the state's population lives between Fort Collins and Pueblo. In Colorado we deal with two things other states do not: mountain-pass weather that can close I-70 in October, and a large volume of partial-year and second-home moves in the ski counties. Most long-distance loads out of Colorado head to Texas, Arizona, and the Pacific Coast; inbound volume still arrives steadily from California and the Midwest.

Pricing

What shapes the price of a Colorado move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Colorado move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Colorado lanes run from about 680 miles to Arizona out to roughly 1,810 miles to Florida. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Full packing is what most long-distance customers choose, and it is the single biggest factor in whether a load arrives intact — a professionally packed box is built for a 2,000-mile ride, not a car trip. Most land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Local notes

Moving from Colorado: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Colorado move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-25 north–south and I-70 east–west carry everything. I-70 through the Eisenhower Tunnel and Vail Pass is the single biggest scheduling variable in the state: chain and traction laws apply from September through May, and a closure can add a full day. Denver's older neighborhoods — Capitol Hill, Baker, Highlands — have alley-only access and narrow streets, and downtown high-rises require freight elevator reservations. Above 8,000 feet, expect a shuttle for many properties. Main corridors: I-25, I-70, I-76, US-285.

STEP 02

Timing your move

June through August is peak and books out early. September and early October are the best window in Colorado: good weather, open availability, and passes still clear. From November through April, any move crossing the Continental Divide carries genuine schedule risk, and we build that into the delivery window rather than promising a date we cannot hold. Spring is the wettest snow season on the Front Range — March blizzards are more common than January ones.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Denver and the northern Front Range absorbed a decade of inbound migration from California, Illinois, and Texas, and the flow has moderated rather than reversed. What has grown is intra-regional outbound — Denver households leaving for Colorado Springs, Boise, Phoenix, and Dallas as Front Range housing costs climbed. Boulder and Fort Collins run distinct university-driven cycles, and the mountain counties are dominated by second-home and seasonal-staff movement.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Colorado are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Colorado Public Utilities Commission, which permits household-goods movers operating within the state — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

Good to know

What catches people out in Colorado.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Colorado move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Mountain pass timing

A move to or from Summit, Eagle, or Pitkin County between November and April should assume at least one weather contingency day. We schedule these loads to cross passes in daylight, which is both safer and faster.

02

Altitude and pressurized items

Aerosols, sealed liquids, and some electronics behave differently between sea level and 5,280 feet. We flag pressurized containers at survey and pack liquids to prevent leaks over the elevation change.

03

Ski-town access windows

Many resort-town HOAs restrict moving trucks during ski season entirely, or limit them to midweek mornings. Vail, Breckenridge, and Aspen properties need building approval well ahead of the date.
