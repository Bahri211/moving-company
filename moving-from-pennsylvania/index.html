What shapes the price of a Pennsylvania move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Pennsylvania move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Pennsylvania lanes run from about 90 miles to New Jersey out to roughly 1,520 miles to Texas. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Packing everything yourself is the cheapest option and full packing is the most expensive; most customers land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Moving from Pennsylvania: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Pennsylvania move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

The Pennsylvania Turnpike, I-81, and I-80 carry cross-state traffic, and the Allegheny Mountains make east–west travel slower than the mileage suggests. Philadelphia rowhouse blocks require a permit to hold curb space and have no driveways. Pittsburgh's hillside neighborhoods — Mount Washington, Polish Hill, the South Side Slopes — have grades and hairpin turns that require shuttles, and some homes are accessible only by public stairway. Main corridors: I-76 (PA Turnpike), I-95, I-79, I-80, I-81, I-83.

STEP 02

Timing your move

Peak is May through August with a State College spike in mid-August. Winter is quiet but the mountain sections of I-80 and the Turnpike see serious snow, and Erie's lake-effect belt is among the snowiest in the country. Philadelphia and Pittsburgh handle winter reasonably well. October is the best month statewide for weather, price, and availability.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

The Poconos, Lehigh Valley, and Bucks County absorb a steady stream of New York and New Jersey households trading commute time for housing cost. Philadelphia gains young professionals and loses families to the suburbs. Pittsburgh has stabilized after decades of decline, gaining on healthcare, robotics, and university employment. Outbound flow from the state runs to Florida, the Carolinas, and Texas, heavily weighted toward retirees.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Pennsylvania are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-18020728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission, which licenses intrastate household-goods movers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

What catches people out in Pennsylvania.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Pennsylvania move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Pittsburgh topography

Some Pittsburgh homes are reached only by a public stairway or a street too steep for a loaded truck. We survey these carefully — the solution is usually a shuttle plus additional crew, and it must be priced in advance.

02

Philadelphia rowhouse permits

Philadelphia rowhouse blocks have no driveways and require a permit to legally reserve curb space for the truck. Without one, expect a long carry down the block.

03

Mountain crossing time

Philadelphia to Pittsburgh is 300 miles but crosses the Alleghenies on the Turnpike, and in winter that is a materially slower and less predictable run than the distance implies.
