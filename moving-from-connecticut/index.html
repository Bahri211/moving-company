Pricing

What your Connecticut move actually costs, and why.

We would rather explain the price than advertise one. A number produced without a survey has nothing behind it, which is how people end up owing more than they were told. These are the factors that move the cost of a Connecticut move — itemized up front, fixed once agreed.

01Distance and lane
Your route sets the floor. Our Connecticut lanes run from about 120 miles to New York out to roughly 1,700 miles to Texas. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.
02Access at both ends
Loading conditions swing the bill more than most people expect. A driveway you can back a fifty-three-foot trailer into is a different job from a third-floor walk-up on a permit-only street. We look at the approach at origin and destination up front so nothing is discovered with the truck already loaded.
03How much packing you want
Packing scope is the lever customers control most directly. Full service is the common choice on interstate lanes because a properly packed carton is the difference between a load that arrives whole and one that doesn't. A split — you do the soft goods, we do glass, art and electronics — is just as workable. Built-to-fit crates for anything oversized are quoted on their own.
04Storage between dates
Few moves go straight from door to door on the same day. Where they don't, we hold the shipment in storage-in-transit under the same inventory, and the cost of those weeks is written into your quote from the start. Climate-controlled space is the sensible choice for wood, leather, and anything with a circuit board.
05Valuation and protection
Coverage is not optional on an interstate move — the federal minimum released-value protection is built into every shipment for free. What you choose is whether to step up to Full Value Protection, which repairs, replaces, or settles at market value and is the sensible call for a high-value load.
Local notes

Leaving Connecticut: the local detail that matters.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Connecticut move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

Commercial vehicles are prohibited on the Merritt and Wilbur Cross parkways, which means moving trucks use I-95 along the shore or I-91 and I-84 inland — a routing detail that adds real time in Fairfield County traffic. Coastal towns from Greenwich to Old Lyme have narrow lanes, stone walls, and low-hanging trees; shuttles are common. Downtown Stamford and New Haven high-rises require certificates of insurance and reserved elevators. Main corridors: I-95, I-91, I-84, Merritt Parkway (CT-15).

STEP 02

Timing your move

Late May through August is peak, tightened further by university turnover in New Haven and Storrs at the end of August. September and October are the state's best combination of weather and availability. Winter moves are quiet but genuinely weather-exposed — a nor'easter can close I-84 and I-91 for a day, and coastal storms bring flooding along the shoreline in Fairfield and New London counties.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Connecticut's net outbound flow is dominated by retirees and remote-capable professionals heading to Florida, the Carolinas, and Tennessee, largely for cost-of-living and tax reasons. Fairfield County runs a partial counter-flow with New York City — households leaving Manhattan and Brooklyn for Greenwich, Darien, and Westport. Hartford's insurance sector and New Haven's university and hospital systems generate steady, smaller inbound corporate volume.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Connecticut are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Connecticut Department of Transportation, which licenses intrastate household-goods carriers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

Good to know

Three Connecticut problems worth knowing about.

Most overruns on a Connecticut move trace back to one of the three below. We ask about all three up front so the price reflects them from the start.

01

No trucks on the Merritt

The parkway restriction is absolute, so any Fairfield County move routes through I-95. On a weekday that can add 45 to 90 minutes each way, which is why we schedule those loads to start early.

02

Historic-district access

New Haven's Wooster Square, Litchfield's green, and much of the shoreline have protected streetscapes with no truck staging. We survey these in advance and plan a shuttle rather than blocking a lane.

03

Snowbird split shipments

A large share of Connecticut outbound moves are seasonal Florida relocations where part of the household stays behind. We can split an inventory between delivery and storage on a single contract.
