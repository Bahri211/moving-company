Pricing

What shapes the price of a Montana move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Montana move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01Distance and lane
Your route sets the floor. Our Montana lanes run from about 480 miles to Idaho out to roughly 1,280 miles to Texas. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.
02Access at both ends
Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.
03How much packing you want
Full packing is what most long-distance customers choose, and it is the single biggest factor in whether a load arrives intact — a professionally packed box is built for a 2,000-mile ride, not a car trip. Most land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.
04Storage between dates
Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.
05Valuation and protection
Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.
Local notes

Moving from Montana: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Montana move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-90 crosses the state east–west and I-15 runs north–south; between them they carry nearly all moving freight. The passes are the scheduling variable — Lookout, Homestake, and MacDonald Pass see chain conditions from October into May. Distances between towns are large enough that a single day of driving may not clear the state. Ranch properties on long unpaved roads and mountain-valley homes routinely require a shuttle. Main corridors: I-90, I-15, I-94, US-93.

STEP 02

Timing your move

The practical Montana moving season is May through October. Winter moves are possible but need a genuinely flexible window: passes close, ground blizzards shut down I-90 and I-15, and unplowed private drives can make a property unreachable for days. June through September is peak for both weather and demand, and Bozeman and Kalispell book out early because crew capacity in the area is limited relative to inbound demand.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Montana became one of the highest net-inbound states per capita during the remote-work shift, with Gallatin County (Bozeman) and Flathead County (Kalispell, Whitefish) taking most of it. Origins skew heavily toward California, Washington, Colorado, and Texas. Outbound volume is modest and mostly young residents leaving for larger job markets. Eastern Montana's population tracks the Bakken energy cycle.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Montana are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-1820728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Montana Department of Transportation Motor Carrier Services division — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

Good to know

What catches people out in Montana.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Montana move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Inbound-heavy imbalance

Far more household goods come into Montana than leave. That means outbound consolidated space is often available at attractive rates, while inbound summer capacity is genuinely scarce and needs early booking.

02

Pass conditions and chain law

From October through May, Montana's mountain passes see chain restrictions and closures. Winter delivery windows here are wider by necessity, and we would rather tell you that than miss a promised date.

03

Ranch and valley access

Long unpaved county roads and steep valley drives are the norm outside town. We survey the last mile and plan a shuttle, which is common enough in Montana that it should be assumed rather than treated as an exception.
