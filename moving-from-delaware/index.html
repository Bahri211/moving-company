What shapes the price of a Delaware move.

We don't publish a price list, because a number without a survey is a guess — and a guess is exactly what turns into a bigger bill on delivery day. Here's what actually moves the figure on a Delaware move. Every one of these is itemized in your written quote before you sign, and the price we agree is the price you pay.

01

Distance and lane

Your route sets the floor. Our Delaware lanes run from about 30 miles to Pennsylvania out to roughly 1,490 miles to Texas. Sharing a trailer with other households costs less than a dedicated truck; a dedicated truck buys you a delivery date instead of a spread.

02

Weight, not bedrooms

Long-distance moves price on what your household actually weighs, not how many rooms it occupies. Two three-bedroom homes can differ by thousands of pounds. This is why we survey by video or in person before quoting rather than guessing from a bedroom count over the phone.

03

Access at both ends

Whether a trailer can reach your door changes the job. Long carries, stairs, freight elevator reservations, street permits, and shuttle service where a tractor-trailer physically cannot fit are all real work — we check them at survey so they land in the quote instead of on move day.

04

How much packing you want

Packing everything yourself is the cheapest option and full packing is the most expensive; most customers land somewhere between, having us handle the kitchen, art, and anything fragile. Custom crating for art, antiques, and oversized items is quoted separately.

05

Storage between dates

Move-out and move-in dates rarely line up. Storage-in-transit holds your inventoried goods until your date, and we quote that cost up front rather than letting it accrue quietly. Climate control is worth it for wood, leather, and electronics.

06

Valuation and protection

Every interstate move includes the federally required minimum released-value protection at no extra charge. Full Value Protection — repair, replacement, or a cash settlement at current market value — costs more and is worth considering for a high-value inventory.

Moving from Delaware: what's actually different.

Every state has its own access problems, weather windows, and rules. Here's what shapes a Delaware move in practice.

STEP 01

Roads & access

I-95 clips the northern tip of the state and DE-1 runs the length of it down to the beaches — that toll road is the practical spine for any Sussex County move. The Chesapeake and Delaware Canal bridges and the Delaware Memorial Bridge are the pinch points for northbound and westbound loads. Beach-town streets in Rehoboth and Bethany are narrow, seasonally congested, and in summer effectively closed to a 53-foot trailer during daylight hours. Main corridors: I-95, DE-1, US-13, US-113.

STEP 02

Timing your move

The beach communities invert the normal calendar: summer is when Sussex County is hardest to move into, because tourist traffic and HOA restrictions limit truck access from Memorial Day to Labor Day. September through November is the best window down there. Northern Delaware follows the standard Mid-Atlantic pattern with a May-through-August peak. Winter is quiet statewide and rarely weather-blocked.

STEP 03

Who is moving, and why

Delaware is one of the strongest net-inbound retiree states on the East Coast, and the reason is explicit: no state sales tax and comparatively low property taxes. Sussex County absorbs households from North Jersey, Long Island, and suburban Philadelphia at a steady clip. Outbound Delaware volume is smaller and mostly heads to Florida and the Carolinas, along with corporate transfers out of Wilmington's financial sector.

STEP 04

Licensing to check

Interstate moves out of Delaware are federal: look for an active USDOT number and FMCSA authority. Ours are USDOT #4575745 and MC-18020728. Movers operating only inside the state answer instead to the Delaware Public Service Commission, which regulates intrastate household-goods carriers — a registration that is not authority to take your belongings across the line.

What catches people out in Delaware.

Three things that regularly turn a straightforward Delaware move into an expensive one. We check all three before quoting, so they land in the price rather than on move day.

01

Beach-town summer restrictions

Rehoboth, Dewey, and Bethany limit or ban commercial trucks on main streets during summer daytime hours. Off-season or early-morning loading is often the only workable option.

02

Two-market state

A Wilmington high-rise move and a Millsboro 55-plus community move need completely different plans — one needs a freight elevator and COI, the other needs gate clearance and weekday-only hours.

03

No sales tax on supplies

A practical footnote: packing materials purchased in Delaware carry no state sales tax, which slightly favors buying your boxes locally rather than across the Pennsylvania line.
